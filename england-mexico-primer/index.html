You know nothing about the team and that's fine. Here's just enough to follow along and not nod at the wrong moments.

01

It's win-or-go-home

The group stage is over. From here it's straight knockout — win and you're in the quarter-finals, lose and you fly home. Extra time and penalties are on the table.

02

England are favourites…

They topped their group and are among the tournament favourites. But they've looked shaky, needing two late Harry Kane goals to scrape past DR Congo last round.

03

…but this is a trap game

It's played in Mexico City, in Mexico's own stadium, at altitude, in front of 80,000 roaring home fans. Mexico haven't conceded a single goal all tournament.

04

England last won in 1966

England have exactly one World Cup, 60 years ago. Every tournament since has been a national exercise in heartbreak. That's where the tension comes from.

The storylines

The drama you need

This is where you win the watch party. Drop any one of these and you'll sound like you've followed it all summer.

A German is in charge of England England

England are managed by Thomas Tuchel — a German. Given the two nations' footballing history, hiring a German to end 60 years of hurt was, let's say, a talking point. He's ruthless, won a Champions League with Chelsea, and now carries the weight of a nation.

The stars he left at home England

Tuchel's squad was called the most shocking in decades. He axed household names — Phil Foden, Cole Palmer and Harry Maguire all missed out. Maguire said he was "shocked and gutted." Big personalities, big absences.

The ghost of the Azteca History

This is the exact stadium where, in 1986, Diego Maradona knocked England out with the infamous "Hand of God" — a goal he punched in with his fist. England haven't played here in 40 years. Painful history hangs over the place. (Video below — essential viewing.)

Mexico's 40-year curse Mexico

Mexico reach the last 16 almost every World Cup, then lose — no quarter-final since 1986. They call the missing step "el quinto partido" (the fifth game). On home soil, this is their best-ever shot to finally break it.

The 80,000-strong 12th man Atmosphere

Fans gathered outside England's hotel through the night, booing and chanting "Mexico" to wreck the players' sleep. The Azteca at altitude is one of football's great fortresses — Mexico have never lost a World Cup match here.

The storm that could stop the game ⚡ Weather

Forecasters put the risk of lightning at kick-off around 90%. FIFA rules force a stoppage if lightning strikes within six miles — so genuinely, the match could be paused or delayed. Keep an eye on the sky.

Kane vs the record books England

Harry Kane arrives red-hot after a monster season at Bayern Munich. He's already England's all-time leading scorer and just became their record World Cup marksman too. One more big night writes more history.
