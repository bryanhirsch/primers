The 60-second brief

This is the last match of the whole tournament. Everything below assumes you know nothing about football — and that's fine, because most of the fun here isn't really about football.

01

This is the actual final

48 countries started a month ago. Two are left. Whoever wins on Sunday is world champion for the next four years. There is no second leg, no next week — it ends Sunday night.

02

Argentina are the holders

They won the last World Cup in 2022. Nobody has won two in a row since 1962 — over 60 years ago. They're one match from doing something genuinely historic.

03

Spain are the form team

Spain have played 7 matches and let in one single goal all tournament. They keep the ball, pass you into exhaustion, and quietly win. They last won this in 2010.

04

The story is Messi

Lionel Messi is 39, widely called the greatest ever, and this is his sixth and final World Cup. He's playing the best football of the tournament. This is the last chapter.

What happened to England

England lost 2–1 to Argentina in the semi-final on Wednesday — they led with 5 minutes to go and threw it away. So the team knocking England out is in Sunday's final. England play France on Saturday at 10pm (BBC One) in the third-place match, which decides nothing but is on anyway.

Football, translated

Football fans speak in code. Here's the code. Once you have these twelve, everything else on this page (and everything the commentators say) makes sense.

Club vs. country
Every player has two teams. Their club is their day job — they're paid by it, in any country. Their country is the national team, based on where they're from. Right now it's the World Cup, so everyone's playing for their country.
Clean sheet
Not letting the other team score at all. "Spain kept a clean sheet" = the other team scored zero.
An assist
The pass that sets up a goal. The scorer gets the glory; the assist is who actually created it. Messi has been doing this rather than scoring lately.
A penalty
A free shot from 12 yards out with only the goalkeeper to beat, awarded when a player is fouled in the area near the goal. Scored roughly 3 times out of 4. Huge moments.
Extra time
A match is 90 minutes. In a knockout match, if it's a draw, they play 30 more minutes. If still drawn, penalties.
Penalty shoot-out
The tie-breaker. Five penalties each, best score wins. Brutal, and how Argentina won the last World Cup.
Stoppage time
Extra minutes added at the end of each half to make up for time wasted. You'll see "90+2" — that means 2 minutes into stoppage time. Argentina's winner against England came here.
The Golden Boot
The prize for the tournament's top goalscorer. Messi has never won it. He's currently leading.
The Ballon d'Or
(Say: ba-LON dor.) The annual award for the best player in the world. Spain's Rodri has one. Messi has won it eight times, more than anyone.
Possession
Which team has the ball more. Spain's whole strategy is to have it constantly so you can't score. It's often 65% – 35% in their favour.
The nicknames
Spain are La Roja ("the red one"). Argentina are La Albiceleste ("the white and sky blue"). Commentators use these constantly instead of the country name.
"Nil"
British for zero. "Two-nil" = 2–0. Nobody says "two-zero." No, there isn't a reason.

The storylines

The drama you need

You don't need to like football to enjoy these. The first one is genuinely one of the strangest coincidences in sport.

Messi once gave Yamal a bath. Seriously. ⭐ The best one

In 2007, a 20-year-old Messi posed for a charity photoshoot for Barcelona — bathing a chubby baby in a tub. That baby was Lamine Yamal, now 19, now Spain's star, and now standing across from Messi in a World Cup final. The photo went viral and fans reckon the bath passed on magic powers. Look it up before Sunday.

Messi's last dance Argentina

Messi is 39 and this is his sixth World Cup — a record — and definitively his last. He has scored more World Cup goals than any player ever, and he's leading the race for top scorer, a prize he has somehow never won. A perfect ending is right there.

Nobody's done this since 1962 History

Argentina won the last World Cup. If they win Sunday, they'll be the first country to win two in a row since Brazil in 1958 and 1962. Winning one is hard enough; defending it has defeated everyone for over 60 years.

Spain's wall Spain

Spain have played seven matches and let in one goal. One. They beat Portugal, Belgium and France on the way here without ever looking rushed. They're not flashy — they're suffocating.

Boring vs. bonkers Styles

Spain win calmly and in control. Argentina have been the opposite — nearly every match a last-gasp escape, including three knockout wins settled in the final minutes. One of these two ways of doing things has to break.

Spain didn't pick a single Real Madrid player Spain

For the first time ever, Spain's squad contains no players from Real Madrid — one of the biggest clubs in the world, in their own country. The coach picked on form alone. In Spain, this is still an argument.

It's basically the Super Bowl now 🎤 Half-time

This final has an American-style half-time show, with Justin Bieber, Madonna, Shakira, BTS and Burna Boy all reportedly performing, plus Post Malone at the closing ceremony. World Cup finals have never done this. Football purists are appalled. Everyone else is delighted.
